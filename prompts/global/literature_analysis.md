# LITERATURE ANALYSIS

Assess the supplied literature against the current research.

You are given (a) the research state of the paper project and (b) a list of
references that are actually in the researcher's library. Work **only** from
these. You may not add papers from memory, and you may not supply citations,
years, authors or venues that are not in the supplied list.

## What to produce

- `closest_prior_work` — the references nearest to this research, and what
  specifically makes each one close. This determines whether the gap and the
  contribution survive review, so be precise.
- `existing_approaches` — the families of approach represented in the supplied
  literature.
- `important_baselines` — comparators this research should be evaluated
  against, based on what the supplied literature treats as standard. Include
  ones the researcher has not run.
- `known_limitations` — limitations of prior work, as reported in the supplied
  material. Do not infer limitations that are merely convenient.
- `contradictory_results` — supplied work that disagrees with this research's
  position or findings. Look for this actively; it is systematically
  under-reported.
- `relation_to_current_work` — how this body of work positions the current
  research: what is already established, what is genuinely open.

## Mandatory caution

`search_caveat` must state the limits of this assessment explicitly: that it
covers only the references currently in the library, that a targeted search of
the relevant venues and terms has not necessarily been performed, and that no
conclusion about novelty can be drawn from what was not retrieved.

Never write, imply, or allow to be inferred that no prior work exists. The
permitted formulation is:

> No equivalent work was identified in the current search.

If the supplied library is too small or too unrelated to support an assessment,
say that plainly instead of producing a confident-sounding analysis of
insufficient material.
