# REVIEW — Results

Assess whether the reported observations are complete, honest about uncertainty,
and consistent.

## Criteria to assess

1. **Observations, not interpretations** — are the results stated as
   measurements, or already framed as conclusions?
2. **Completeness** — is every experiment's outcome reported, including for
   conditions where the method presumably did worse? Compare the set of
   experiments against the set of results and name any experiment with no
   reported outcome. Silent gaps in a results grid are a serious concern.
3. **Uncertainty** — is variability reported? Number of runs, spread, intervals?
   Differences discussed without any measure of variability cannot be
   distinguished from noise, especially when small.
4. **Negative results preserved** — are results that do not support the
   hypothesis reported as prominently as those that do? If a negative result
   appears in the raw material but disappears from the summary, raise it.
5. **Statistical claims** — is any language of significance, reliability or
   consistency backed by an actual test in the supplied material? Words like
   "significantly", "substantially" and "consistently" used without evidence are
   an issue in their own right.
6. **Consistency across experiments** — do results agree with each other? Are
   the same conditions reported with the same values wherever they appear?
7. **Magnitude in context** — is the size of the difference meaningful relative
   to the run-to-run variation, and relative to what matters in this field?

## Section-specific failure modes to look for

- Improvements within the range of seed variance presented as effects.
- Selective reporting: best seed, best epoch, or best subset of conditions.
- Metrics changing between comparisons.
- "Significant" used colloquially, then relied upon as if statistical.
- A results table with conspicuously absent cells.
- Results reported only as relative improvements, with absolute values omitted.

Do not perform statistical tests yourself and do not estimate significance. If a
statistical claim lacks evidence, the action is for the researcher to supply the
test or remove the claim.
