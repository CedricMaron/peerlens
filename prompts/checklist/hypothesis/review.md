# REVIEW — Hypothesis

Assess whether each hypothesis is falsifiable, measurable, scoped, and
distinguishable from its alternatives.

## Criteria to assess

1. **Falsifiability** — is there an observation that would refute it? State that
   observation concretely in your reason. If no outcome could refute it, that is
   at least a `major` issue.
2. **Measurable variables** — are the independent and dependent variables
   identifiable and operationalisable? Is the measurement defined, or only the
   concept? ("knowledge transfer" is a concept; "global model top-1 accuracy" is
   a measurement.)
3. **Scope stated** — are the conditions under which it should hold specified?
   Unscoped hypotheses produce unscoped claims.
4. **Assumptions surfaced** — what must be true for the prediction to follow?
   Are those assumptions themselves tested, or taken for granted?
5. **Competing explanations** — what *else* would produce the predicted
   observation? Enumerate at least the most plausible alternative explicitly.
   This is the single most valuable check in this section.
6. **Mechanism vs. effect** — if a causal mechanism is proposed, is there a
   prediction that discriminates that mechanism from a purely correlational or
   incidental account? A hypothesis that predicts only "our method wins" cannot
   establish why.
7. **Supporting/refuting evidence identified** — does the research state what
   evidence would support and what would reject the hypothesis, before looking
   at the results?
8. **Consistency with the question** — does confirming or refuting the
   hypothesis answer the research question?

## Section-specific failure modes to look for

- The hypothesis is a restatement of the method's expected behaviour, so it
  cannot fail without the method simply being broken.
- Directional prediction with no scope, licensing universal claims later.
- The proposed mechanism is untestable with the planned experiments, but is
  nevertheless the thing the contribution will claim.
- A hypothesis so vague ("improves performance") that almost any result
  confirms it.
- Multiple mechanisms bundled so that support for one is presented as support
  for all.

For every hypothesis, name at least one concrete alternative explanation, and
state whether the research as it stands can rule it out. If it cannot, that is
an issue, and its severity depends on whether the main claim rests on the
mechanism.
