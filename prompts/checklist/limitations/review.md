# REVIEW — Limitations

Assess whether the limitations honestly bound what the research concludes.

The test for this section is not length. It is whether a reader who takes the
limitations seriously would still accept the claims.

## Criteria to assess

1. **Assumptions surfaced** — are the assumptions the method and analysis rest
   on stated? Untested assumptions treated as given are limitations.
2. **Limits of interpretation stated** — for each main claim, is the boundary of
   what it can mean made explicit?
3. **Untested conditions** — are the conditions the claims range over but the
   experiments never covered listed?
4. **External validity** — is it clear where these results should and should not
   be expected to transfer? Is that judgement evidenced or asserted?
5. **Unresolved uncertainty** — are known confounds, noise, and ambiguous
   results acknowledged rather than resolved by assertion?
6. **Coverage of real weaknesses** — compare this section against the issues
   raised in methodology, experiments, results, findings and contribution. Any
   material weakness identified there and absent here is itself an issue: the
   limitations are performing reassurance rather than disclosure.
7. **Non-genericness** — are these the actual limits of *this* study?

## Section-specific failure modes to look for

- Generic filler: "more datasets could be tested", "future work could explore
  larger scales", "results may not generalise to all settings" — flag these as
  non-informative *unless* the claims genuinely depend on the untested extent,
  in which case restate them concretely and connect them to the specific claim.
- Limitations listed that do not constrain any claim, while the ones that would
  constrain the headline claim are absent.
- A serious confound described as an "interesting direction for future work".
- Limitations that are actually strengths in disguise ("our method is so general
  that...").
- Acknowledging a limitation in this section while the abstract and conclusion
  continue to claim as if it did not exist. Check that consistency directly.

A limitations section is `ready` when it names the genuine boundaries of the
work, connects them to specific claims, and contains nothing that exists merely
to appear humble.
